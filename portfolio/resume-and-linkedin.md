# Resume lines, a LinkedIn post, and your interview story

## Resume lines

Use this shape: **did what, for whom, measured how, by doing what.** One project gets two or three lines.
Say if the client was simulated.

- <Built / Shipped / Reduced> <what> for <client>, <result with a number>, by <how>.
- <Designed / Tested> <what>, <result>, using <how>.

**Example (Northstar):**

- Built a WhatsApp admissions agent for a simulated skills institute, cutting the median night-time reply from
  about 10 hours to seconds on a synthetic week of 420 chats, using retrieval, tool calling and safe booking.
- Designed a 10-case eval set and guardrails that block wrong fees, job promises and phone numbers; raised the
  pass rate from 8/10 to 10/10, with every high-risk case passing.
- Ran discovery with simulated stakeholders, scored four options on value, data, effort and risk, and wrote the
  scope doc and launch plan (shadow mode, night-only rollout, one-switch rollback).

Avoid: lists of tools with no result ("Python, LangChain, RAG, vector DB"), and claims you can't explain in detail.

## LinkedIn post

**Template:**

> <One line: what you built and the result.>
>
> <Two or three lines: the problem, and the most interesting thing you found.>
>
> <One line: the hardest part, and what you learned.>
>
> <Link to the repo and the demo video.>

**Example:**

> I built a WhatsApp admissions agent for a simulated skills institute. On a synthetic week of 420 chats,
> night enquiries got a reply in seconds instead of about 10 hours.
>
> The surprise: the hardest problem wasn't the AI. The institute had two fee sheets that disagreed, and the agent
> quoted both until I fixed the source.
>
> The lesson: the rarest failure, three missed handovers to a person, mattered more than the 18 misunderstood messages.
>
> Repo and 3-minute demo: <link>

## Your two-minute interview story

Four parts, as in Lesson 12: the problem, what you did, the result as a number, what you learned. Practise it out
loud until it takes two minutes. Expect follow-up questions on any line: be ready to show the code and the numbers.
