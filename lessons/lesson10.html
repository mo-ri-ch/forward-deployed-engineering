Talking to the client

The weekly update

Clients get nervous when they hear nothing. Send a short update at the same time every week, even when little changed.

Four parts, a line each: what's done, what's next, what's blocked, and one number that shows progress.

Write this Friday's update to Priya

Updates say it. Demos show it.

Demos that land

A demo isn't a tour of features. It shows the client their own problem being solved, with their own data.

Start with the problem they told you about, show it happen and get solved, show the number, then ask what would stop them using it.

Put the demo in order

Sometimes the news is bad.

Bad news early

WhatsApp hasn't approved Northstar's business account yet, so the launch will slip a week.

Bad news gets worse with time. Tell the client as soon as you know: what happened, the new date, and what you're doing about it. No blame, no excuses.

When the scope grows

Once a client sees something working, the requests come: "Can it also…?" New work quietly added to an agreed project is called scope creep. It's how projects end up late and half-done.

Don't say a flat no. Say what it would cost, and offer a choice.

Whatever you write, keep it short.

Write for busy people

Priya reads your email on her phone, between meetings. Put the point in the subject and the first line. Ask for one thing, with a date.

If she has to read twice to find out what you need, it waits.
