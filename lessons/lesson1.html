Meet your client

A different kind of engineer

Most software engineers build one product that many customers use.

Some engineers work the other way round. They go to one customer, learn how that business works, and build what it needs, often with AI. This job is called a forward deployed engineer, or FDE.

Half of an FDE's day is code. The other half is the customer. Step through one day:

A day as an FDE
  1. 09:00Call with the customer's support team: what went wrong yesterday?customer
  2. 10:00Read the customer's data: 3,000 chat messages.code
  3. 11:30Fix a bug in code the customer's own team wrote.code
  4. 14:00Build the next piece of the AI system.code
  5. 16:30Show the customer what changed, and listen to what they think.customer
  6. 17:30Send a short update: what's done, what's next, what's stuck.customer

So what skills does that take?

Two skill sets

That day needs two different skill sets.

The first is engineering: writing and fixing code, often code someone else wrote, on data that is messy.

The second is looking after the customer: finding out what they really need, agreeing what gets built, keeping promises and explaining clearly.

You need both. Brilliant code that solves the wrong problem is wasted.

Why are companies hiring so many people for this?

Why companies want FDEs

Companies are buying AI tools fast. But a tool doesn't fit a company's messy data and habits by itself. Someone has to make it work inside each company.

That's why FDE job listings grew about eight times over in 2025.
