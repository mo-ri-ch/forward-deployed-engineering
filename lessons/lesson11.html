You'll need data.

Make realistic data

You won't have a client's real data, and you must never use real people's details. So you make realistic data that looks real but isn't, called synthetic data.

A model can help you make lots of it. But real data is messy, so plant the mess on purpose, like the two fee sheets and duplicate leads you met at Northstar.

Then plan your four weeks.

Plan four weeks

Give yourself four weeks, about 10 hours a week. That's the same shape as a real first engagement, just smaller.

Most people spend too long building and too little testing. The plan below protects testing time.

Step through the four weeks
  1. week 1Discovery: read the brief, list questions, make the data, write the scope doc.
  2. week 2Build the smallest version that works from start to finish.
  3. week 3Eval set, guardrails, and fixing what the evals find.
  4. week 4Run it on a test group, measure the metric, record the demo, write it up.

Check your time budget.

Remember:
hours = weeks × hours per week
part = share × total
1. Four weeks at 10 hours a week. How many hours do you have?
2. If every week gets the same time, how many hours go to evals and guardrails?
3. Your eval set has 30 cases and your bar is 90%. How many must pass?

How is it judged?

How it's judged

Your capstone is judged the way a client and an interviewer would judge it: did you find the real problem, did you prove it works, and can you explain it?

PartStrong
ProblemOne clear problem, backed by data, with a metric and baseline
BuildSmall, works start to finish, readable code
ProofAn eval set with results, and guardrails for the risky cases
ResultThe metric before and after
StoryA short write-up and demo a stranger understands

Last step: show it.

Present it

Record a demo video of about three minutes. Most people who look at your project will watch this, and nothing else.

Use the same shape as a client demo: the problem, their data, it working, the number, and what you'd do next.
