Discovery

Vague words hide questions

An FDE's first week with a client is called discovery: finding the real problem before building anything.

It starts with the brief. A phrase like "things feel chaotic" sounds clear, but you can't build anything from it. It hides a question.

Turn each vague phrase into a question whose answer is a number or an example.

    What do you ask them?

    Ask questions that open up

    An interview only helps if people tell you what really happens.

    A question that opens up asks them to describe or show: "Walk me through what happens when a message comes in at night."

    A leading question hands them your answer to agree with: "Wouldn't a chatbot fix this?" People are polite, so they say yes, and you learn nothing.

    Score the options

    The tree points to four things you could build. Mark each one from 1 to 5 on: value to Northstar, how ready the data is, the effort to build it, and the risk if it goes wrong.

    Value and ready data are good, so they add. Effort and risk are costs, so they take away:
    score = value + data − effort − risk

    For the admissions agent: 5 + 4 − 3 − 2 = 4.

    Change any mark you disagree with

    How will you know it worked?

    Pick the number that proves it

    The number that shows whether a project worked is its success metric.

    A good one passes three checks: you can measure it today, the sponsor cares about it, and what you build can change it directly.

    Careful: Arjun's team is judged on calls booked, but Priya cares about students who join. They're not the same.

    Which metric would you pick for the admissions agent?

    ✏️ Practice 2.2

    Remember:
    score = value + data − effort − risk
    The median is the middle value once the values are in order. It isn't pulled up by one very slow case.
    1. A new idea: a bot that reminds students when an EMI payment is due. Value 2, data 5, effort 1, risk 1. What's its score?
    2. Five night enquiries waited 620, 540, 690, 480 and 720 minutes for a first reply. What's the median wait, in minutes?
    3. The target is a first reply within 5 minutes. How many times faster than a 620-minute wait is that?

    Today's value of a metric, like the 620 minutes, is called the baseline. You measure it before you build, so you can show the change after.

    Write the scope doc

    Everything you found goes on one page: the problem, who it's for, the success metric, what the first version does and doesn't do, and the data it uses.

    That page is a scope doc. The client agrees to it before you build, so nobody is surprised later.

    The hardest part is saying what you won't do.

    Pick the best line for each part, then check

    Now convince the client.

    Pitch it to Priya

    You present the scope doc to Priya. A good sponsor pushes back, because it's her money and her team.

    Good answers are honest, short and use the numbers you found. Don't promise what you can't keep.
